UI/UX Design Concept Project

Nanora

Trusted care. Meaningful learning.

A two-sided childcare marketplace connecting families with vetted nanny-tutors and specialized caregivers — designed around trust, clarity, and meaningful care experiences for children ages 1–5.

Project overview

Nanora is a childcare platform concept that bridges the gap between families seeking reliable childcare and qualified caregivers looking for meaningful work. The project explored the full spectrum of UX/UI design — from initial concept development through information architecture to visual design direction.

Role

UX/UI Designer

Deliverables

Concept document, sitemap, user journeys, visual design system

Tools

Figma, Miro, Adobe Illustrator

Problem statement

Parents face significant challenges finding childcare that is safe, verified, and responsive to their child's developmental needs. Existing platforms often lack transparency in caregiver vetting, offer limited filtering for specialized care needs, and fail to build the trust that families require before inviting someone into their home.

"Trust first, clarity second, convenience third." — Core design principle

Goals and objectives

  • Create a trust-centered platform where safety and verification are visible at every touchpoint
  • Design for two distinct user groups — families and caregivers — with tailored experiences
  • Support three core care types: nanny-tutors, specialized care, and respite care
  • Build an information architecture that makes discovery and comparison intuitive
  • Align with UNICEF Early Childhood Development frameworks for nurturing care

Design process

The design process followed a structured approach starting with user research and concept development, moving through information architecture, and arriving at visual design decisions.

Core user journey

1

Tell us what you need

Child age, care type, schedule, location, special requirements

2

Discover caregivers

Filter by skills, experience, availability, care type

3

Compare & understand

Review profiles, verification badges, reviews, care philosophy

4

Request or book

Send inquiries or booking requests based on availability

5

Manage care

Schedule management, messaging, care notes, support

Information architecture

The website was structured around a 12-page sitemap designed to serve both audience groups while maintaining clear navigation paths:

Core Pages

  • Home — value proposition & care types
  • Find a Caregiver — searchable directory
  • Caregiver Profile — credentials & reviews
  • Services — detailed care type explanations

Trust & Onboarding

  • How It Works — 4-step journey
  • Safety & Trust — vetting protocol
  • For Caregivers — recruitment landing
  • About Nanora — mission & story

Support & Access

  • Resources / Blog
  • FAQ
  • Contact / Support
  • Login / Sign Up

Visual design direction

The visual language was developed to communicate warmth, safety, and professionalism — balancing the institutional trust required in childcare with the human warmth that families seek.

Deep Comfort Teal Trust · Stability
Soft Coral Warmth · Approachability
Warm Cream Calm · Welcoming

Typography uses rounded humanistic sans-serifs to create a friendly yet clear reading experience. UI components feature soft rounded cards, generous whitespace, and high-contrast accessible elements.

Challenges and learnings

  • Designing for trust: Understanding how to communicate safety through visual cues, content hierarchy, and verification systems without making the experience feel clinical.
  • Two-sided marketplace: Balancing the needs of two distinct user groups (families and caregivers) within a cohesive experience.
  • Information density: Structuring complex caregiver profiles with credentials, reviews, and availability without overwhelming users.
  • Emotional design: Creating an interface that handles the emotional sensitivity of childcare decisions with appropriate care and reassurance.
